\begin{abstract}
RLPD obtains strong offline-to-online performance with a large $N$-head critic
ensemble and min-over-$M$ target subsetting, yet \emph{why} the ensemble helps
is unresolved. The standard pessimism and head-diversity accounts leave a
puzzle that neither resolves: dropout substitutes for ensemble members at small
$N$ but adds nothing at large $N$. We propose that the operative property is the
\emph{action-space smoothness of the ensemble-mean critic}, the function the
actor's gradient actually climbs. We measure it as a $Q$-scale-normalized
sharpness $\nsharp$, the variance of $\Sbar(s,a{+}\epsilon)$ under small action
perturbations, normalized by $|\Sbar|^2$. Across a multi-seed grid on sparse
Adroit manipulation (pen, door; $N{\in}\{2,4,6,10\}$, dropout
$p{\in}\{0,0.01\}$, with min-free $M{=}1$ ablations),
\prov{dropout reduces $\nsharp$ and improves score only at small $N$, and the
benefit decays monotonically in $N$ along a saturation curve, reconciling DroQ's
small-ensemble gains with the absence of a dropout benefit at large $N$}, while
\prov{the min-free failure is an amplitude event: $|\Sbar|$ inflates by
${\sim}\MOneQabsRatio\times$ with no accompanying rise in normalized sharpness,
which separates pessimism from geometry}. We then add a pre-registered TD3-style
target-policy-smoothing arm, an action-space knob that adds no ensemble
diversity, to test the central prediction of dose-dependent gains at small $N$.
\prov{It did not reproduce dropout's effect, and because it moved measured
sharpness only marginally we report it as inconclusive}. Finally,
\prov{across non-divergent configurations $\nsharp$ is associated with final
score (config-level $\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRhoCfgExclM$, permutation
$p{=}\ProspectivePermPExclM$, $n{=}\ProspectiveNCfgExclM$ configs), a weak early
signal that is not yet a practical selection rule}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RLPD~\citep{ball2023efficient} is a standard recipe for online RL with offline
data: symmetric offline/online sampling, LayerNorm in the critic, a large
$N$-head $Q$-ensemble, and targets formed by a min over a random $M$-of-$N$
head subset. The sampling scheme and the LayerNorm have well-understood
rationales. The ensemble is murkier, and its benefit is usually narrated
through two mechanisms, both of which run into a simple consistency check.

\textbf{Pessimism.} Min-subsetting controls pessimism, and pessimism clearly
matters here: with $M{=}1$ (no min) and no other regularizer, training diverges
on sparse Adroit (\S\ref{sec:interventions}). Pessimism is governed by $M$
rather than $N$, though. At fixed $M{=}2$ the expected subset minimum barely
changes with $N$, yet performance climbs monotonically in $N$
(\S\ref{sec:score}), so the knob that drives performance is a different one
from the knob that sets pessimism.

\textbf{Head diversity.} A second family of explanations credits disagreement
among heads: ensembles work because heads are independent and
diverse~\citep{ghasemipour2022msg,an2021edac,lee2023spqr}, and DroQ
\citep{hiraoka2022droq} reads dropout as an implicit ensemble in this spirit.
The consistency check fails in both directions. Dropout at $N{=}2$, which is
implicit averaging inside just two heads, \prov{recovers most of the
large-ensemble performance}, while the same mechanism at $N{=}10$ adds diversity
of the same kind and \prov{changes nothing}. (RLPD found dropout an inferior
\emph{substitute} for ensembling on sparse Adroit; that it is also inert when
\emph{added on top} of a large ensemble is what our pre-registered multi-seed
grid establishes.) Diversity goes up while performance stays flat.

The property that does track performance is geometric: the
\emph{smoothness in action space of the ensemble-mean critic} $\Sbar$. The
actor never sees individual heads; its update ascends
$\nabla_a \Sbar(s,\cdot)$. When $\Sbar$ is locally sharp, with high curvature
and spurious local peaks, actor gradients climb toward those peaks; when it is
smooth they track the genuine value landscape. Ensemble
averaging, dropout averaging, and target
smoothing then become candidate routes to one shared resource, and that view
predicts saturation directly: once $\Sbar$ is smooth, a further smoother
changes little.

The critic's action-space geometry has been recognised as central to the
actor's update in recent critic-smoothing methods
\citep{lee2026pave,jain2025savo} and smoothness-regularized offline-to-online
RL \citep{wen2024ro2o}. Our contribution is using it as a
\emph{diagnostic} rather than adding a new regularizer. We ask whether the
RLPD ensemble's specific benefit \emph{is} this smoothing, and we
\emph{dissociate} that smoothing from the pessimism and head-diversity accounts
the ensemble literature invokes.

We measure \emph{normalized sharpness} $\nsharp$, the variance of
$\Sbar(s, a{+}\epsilon)$ under small Gaussian action perturbations, normalized
by squared mean $|\Sbar|$ so that value rescaling cannot masquerade as
geometry (\S\ref{sec:metric}). On sparse Adroit pen and door
(offline-to-online, 1M steps), across $N{\in}\{2,4,6,10\}$,
$M{\in}\{1,2\}$, dropout $p{\in}\{0,0.01\}$, with 3--5 seeds per
claim-bearing configuration, we find:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{Interaction.} \prov{Dropout improves final score at small $N$
  and is inert at large $N$, decaying monotonically in $N$}
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}; pen $\Delta{=}\PenNTwoDelta\!\to\!\PenNTenDelta$
  across $N{=}2{\to}10$), and \prov{the same pattern appears in $\nsharp$}
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:sharp}a). On pen the $N{=}2$ vs $N{=}10$ deltas are fully
  separated (a post-hoc descriptive contrast); door is directionally
  consistent but not significant ($n{=}3$). This reconciles DroQ's gains with
  the absence of a large-$N$ dropout benefit as one saturation curve.
  \item \textbf{Dissociation from pessimism and diversity.} \prov{The min-free
  ($M{=}1$) failure is an amplitude effect: $|\Sbar|$ inflates by
  ${\sim}\MOneQabsRatio\times$ on the probe set while normalized sharpness stays
  flat} (\S\ref{sec:interventions}). \prov{Final $\nsharp$ is associated with
  final score across configurations, but only weakly} (config-level
  $\rho_s{=}\SharpRhoCfg$, permutation $p{=}\SharpPermP$;
  $\rho_s{=}\SharpRhoCfgExclM$ excluding the divergent $M{=}1$ arms), so the
  dissociation rests mainly on the designed diversity contrasts, with the
  correlation in a supporting role.
  \item \textbf{Intervention (pre-registered, inconclusive).} TD3-style
  target-policy smoothing \citep{fujimoto2018td3} smooths targets in action
  space without changing ensemble size, dropout, or the function class. Its
  pre-registered prediction was dose-dependent gains at $N{=}2$ and none at
  $N{=}10$. \prov{It did not reproduce dropout's effect, and it moved measured
  sharpness only marginally, so we report it as inconclusive}
  (\S\ref{sec:interventions}).
  \item \textbf{Prospective value.} \prov{$\nsharp$ at the 100k-step checkpoint
  is associated with final score among non-divergent pen configurations
  (config-level $\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRhoCfgExclM$, permutation
  $p{=}\ProspectivePermPExclM$, $n{=}\ProspectiveNCfgExclM$; pooling all
  configurations the association is weaker and not significant,
  $\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRhoCfg$, $p{=}\ProspectivePermP$)}, a weak early
  signal that falls short of a practical selection rule (\S\ref{sec:prospective}).
\end{enumerate}

We contribute no new algorithm. The paper is a pre-registered, falsifiable
attempt to isolate which property of the RLPD ensemble recipe tracks
performance in offline-to-online RL, separating smoothness from pessimism and
diversity, and reporting the interventions, controls, and limits alongside.

\section{Preliminaries and the sharpness metric}
\label{sec:metric}

\paragraph{RLPD critic update.} RLPD trains a SAC-style agent
\citep{haarnoja2018sac} with an $N$-head critic
$\{Q_{\theta_i}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ (LayerNorm in each head). For a minibatch
$\mathcal{B}=\{(s_j,a_j,r_j,s'_j)\}_{j=1}^{B}$ mixing offline and online
samples 50/50, targets use a fresh random subset
$\mathcal{M}_j\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}$, $|\mathcal{M}_j|{=}M$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:td}
y_j \;=\; r_j + \gamma\Big(\min_{i\in\mathcal{M}_j}
Q_{\bar\theta_i}(s'_j, a'_j) \;-\; \alpha\log\pi_\phi(a'_j\,|\,s'_j)\Big),
\qquad a'_j\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\,|\,s'_j),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{NB}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{B}
\big(Q_{\theta_i}(s_j,a_j) - y_j\big)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $\bar\theta$ are EMA target parameters. Every head regresses to the
same target $y_j$. Ensemble size $N$, subset size $M$, and (optional)
per-head dropout with independent masks all act inside
Eqs.~\ref{eq:td}--\ref{eq:loss}; the actor update is untouched and ascends the
ensemble mean $\Sbar(s,a)=\frac1N\sum_i Q_{\theta_i}(s,a)$.

\paragraph{Normalized sharpness.} Fix a probe set
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{probe}}$ of 1{,}000 $(s,a)$ pairs drawn once from the
offline dataset. For perturbation scale $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp}
S_\sigma \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{probe}}}\,
\mathrm{Var}_{\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)}
\big[\,\Sbar(s, a+\epsilon)\,\big],
\qquad
\nsharp \;=\; \frac{S_\sigma}
{\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{probe}}}|\Sbar(s,a)|\big)^{2}} .
\end{equation}
A second-order expansion gives
$S_\sigma\approx\sigma^2\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla_a\Sbar(s,a)\|^2$: $S_\sigma$ is
mean squared action-gradient magnitude, the same functional form as
parameter-space sharpness \citep{keskar2016large,foret2021sam} transported to
the space the \emph{actor} optimizes over. The normalization matters here:
under value rescaling $\Sbar\!\to\!c\Sbar$, raw $S_\sigma$ scales as $c^2$
with identical geometry, so any configuration that inflates $|Q|$ (e.g.\ the
$M{=}1$ divergence) inflates raw sharpness for free. $\nsharp$ is invariant to
$c$ and measures landscape shape alone. We estimate
Eq.~\ref{eq:sharp} with 100 perturbations per pair, dropout disabled and
deterministic forward passes, every 50k steps, at
$\sigma\in\{0.01,0.05,0.1\}$ with independent perturbation seeds per scale;
$\sigma{=}0.05$ is the pre-registered headline scale and the other two form a
robustness sweep (App.~\ref{app:sigma}). Actions are normalized to
$[-1,1]^d$, so $\sigma$ is 1--10\% of the action half-range. Probe points
with $|\Sbar|<1$ are masked to keep the denominator meaningful (in practice
only step 0).

\paragraph{Diversity metrics (for comparison).} Head variance
$\mathrm{Var}_i[Q_{\theta_i}(s,a)]$ and, for dropout runs, mask variance
$\mathrm{Var}_k[\Sbar(s,a;\mathrm{mask}_k)]$ (abusing notation: $\Sbar$
evaluated under dropout mask $k$), both averaged over the same
probe set. These are the quantities a diversity account says should matter.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Tasks and protocol.} Sparse-reward Adroit manipulation
\citep{rajeswaran2018dapg,fu2020d4rl}: \texttt{pen-binary-v0} (horizon 100)
and \texttt{door-binary-v0} (horizon 200), the regime where RLPD's ensemble
is known to help and where critic $Q$-landscapes are prone to spurious peaks. Reward is $-1$
per step until success, $0$ after; we report scores as the fraction of the
evaluation horizon spent in success, i.e.\ $1+\bar{R}/H\in[0,1]$, averaged
over 20 evaluation episodes every 5k steps for 1M environment steps.
\textbf{Grid.} At $M{=}2$: $N\in\{2,4,6,10\}\times p\in\{0,0.01\}$ on both
environments, 3 seeds per cell, with 5 seeds on the four pen headline cells
($N\in\{2,10\}\times p$), giving 32 pen + 24 door runs. At $M{=}1$ (pen,
$N{=}2$): $p\in\{0,0.01\}$, 3 seeds each, for 6 runs; 62 in total, plus a
24-run TD3-style target-policy-smoothing (TPS) arm
(\S\ref{sec:interventions}). All runs use
one harness with the probe compiled in, so every run carries both score and
sharpness; no results are mixed across harness versions (the mostly single-seed
April-era runs of the course report are quarantined to a replication check,
App.~\ref{app:replication}).
\textbf{Statistics (pre-registered).} Seeds are aggregated as median with
min--max bands (never mean$\pm$SEM at $n{\le}5$); cross-config consistency
uses exact binomial sign tests with one unit per config; same-seed pairing is
used for dropout contrasts only, since TPS arms consume an extra RNG split and
are compared as distributions (Mann--Whitney), never seed-paired.
``Final score'' is the mean of a run's last 10 evaluations (fixed in the
harness before launch); ``final sharpness'' is the median of the probes in
the last 200k steps. The full discipline, fixed before fleet completion,
ships with the analysis code.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The dropout--ensemble interaction on score}
\label{sec:score}

Figure~\ref{fig:headline} shows the score grid (full numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:headline}, learning curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:curves});
Table~\ref{tab:signtests} gives the pre-registered tests. We read off three
patterns. \textbf{(i)} \prov{Without dropout, final score increases with $N$}
($N{=}10$ vs.\ $N{=}2$: \NEffectPairs{} concordant seed pairs, descriptive,
since the formal tests are the config-level dropout contrasts). \textbf{(ii)}
\prov{Dropout at $N{=}2$ closes most of that gap} (pen: median
$\Delta{=}\DropDeltaPenNTwo$). \textbf{(iii)} \prov{Dropout at $N{=}10$ is
inert} (pen: $\Delta{=}\DropDeltaPenNTen$). The benefit \prov{decays
monotonically with $N$: the pen dropout $\Delta$ is
$\PenNTwoDelta, \PenNFourDelta, \PenNSixDelta, \PenNTenDelta$ at
$N{=}2,4,6,10$} (Table~\ref{tab:headline}), the saturation curve a
single-resource account predicts. \prov{The $N{=}2$ and $N{=}10$ per-seed
deltas are fully separated on pen} (a post-hoc descriptive contrast rather than
a pre-registered test; door is directionally consistent at $n{=}3$). The
\emph{pre-registered, pooled} dropout sign tests (Table~\ref{tab:signtests}) are
null by design: dropout helps only at small $N$, so pooling with the inert
larger-$N$ cells washes the main effect out. Our claim concerns the
$N$-interaction (the dose curve); we make no claim about a dropout main effect. This is
the DroQ/RLPD discrepancy retested inside one controlled grid.

Figure~\ref{fig:sharp}a shows $\nsharp$ during training:
\prov{ensemble growth and dropout both reduce the sharpness of $\Sbar$, with
the same saturation structure as the score plot}. The cross-config association
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sharp}b) is real but weak once seeds are not double-counted:
\prov{at the config level final $\nsharp$ correlates with final score with
$\rho_s{=}\SharpRhoCfg$ (permutation $p{=}\SharpPermP$, $n{=}\SharpNCfg$
configs), strengthening to $\rho_s{=}\SharpRhoCfgExclM$ (perm $p{=}\SharpPermPExclM$)
when the divergent $M{=}1$ arms are excluded}; the pooled per-run figure $\rho_s{=}\SharpRho$
($n{=}\SharpRhoN$ runs) is anticonservative, counting 3--5 correlated seeds
per config as independent. The dissociation from diversity therefore leans on
the designed contrasts, where the accounts disagree by construction:
\prov{$N{=}2{+}$dropout (little diversity) and $N{=}10$ plain reach near-comparable
score despite very different head counts and mask diversity (a residual gap
remains, Limitation~(iv)), and $N{=}10{+}$dropout adds mask diversity but
neither smooths $\Sbar$ further nor scores higher}. The April-era measured
diversity diagnostics (quarantined to App.~\ref{app:diversity}) corroborate
but do not carry this claim.

\subsection{Two interventions: pessimism and smoothness are separate axes}
\label{sec:interventions}

\textbf{Min-$Q$ subsetting (pessimism axis).} At $N{=}2$ on pen, removing the
min ($M{=}1$, no dropout) \prov{sends $|\Sbar|$ on the probe set up by orders
of magnitude (median $|\Sbar|{\approx}\MOneQabs$ vs.\ ${\approx}\HealthyQabs$
for healthy runs, a ${\sim}\MOneQabsRatio\times$ inflation) and the run collapses},
the classic signature of unchecked overestimation (Fig.~\ref{fig:minq}, App.).
The informative part is what stays fixed.
\prov{The collapse lives in amplitude while geometry stays put: normalized
sharpness does \emph{not} rise with it (its $M{=}1$ median is
$\MOneSharpRatio\times$ the healthy median, an order of magnitude \emph{below}),
even as $|\Sbar|$ leaves the healthy range by three orders of magnitude}.
Pessimism is necessary on sparse Adroit, where it prevents divergence, and it is
a separate resource from smoothness: $M$ sets amplitude control while $N$ and
dropout set geometry. This is also why the normalization in Eq.~\ref{eq:sharp}
matters, since raw sharpness would have ``tracked'' the $M{=}1$ collapse purely
through the $c^2$ amplitude artifact. The design also includes
$M{=}1{+}$dropout arms (Table~\ref{tab:headline}): \prov{dropout rescues the
min-free collapse (median score \PenMOneNoDrop{} $\to$ \PenMOneDrop)}, so the
min is not the only route to amplitude control, which fits the
smoothness account if the overestimation spiral feeds on the spurious sharp
maxima that smoothing removes. As pre-registered, we read the two axes off
$|\Sbar|$ and $\nsharp$ together, never inferring them from score alone.

\textbf{Target-policy smoothing (smoothness axis).} If smoothness of $\Sbar$
is the operative resource, then smoothing it by a route that adds \emph{no
ensemble diversity at all} should reproduce dropout's effect pattern. We add
TD3-style target-policy smoothing \citep{fujimoto2018td3}: clipped Gaussian
noise on the target action in Eq.~\ref{eq:td},
$a'_j \mathrel{+}= \mathrm{clip}(\epsilon, \pm 2.5\sigma_{\mathrm{TPS}})$,
$\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{TPS}}^2)$ (distinct from the probe
perturbation of Eq.~\ref{eq:sharp}), leaving the entropy term evaluated at the
unperturbed action. This convolves targets with a kernel in action space and
adds no new heads, masks, or function-class constraint. The pre-registered
prediction, fixed before the runs, was dose-dependent gains at $N{=}2$ (sharp
regime) and little change at $N{=}10$ (already smooth).
\prov{The prediction did not bear out: across
$\sigma_{\mathrm{TPS}}\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ score is flat-to-worse at $N{=}2$ and
unchanged at $N{=}10$} (distribution comparison vs.\
$\sigma_{\mathrm{TPS}}{=}0$: $N{=}2$ $p{=}\TpsMWPenNTwo$, $N{=}10$
$p{=}\TpsMWPenNTen$; Fig.~\ref{fig:tps}, Table~\ref{tab:tps}). We read this
as \emph{inconclusive} rather than as a refutation, for a concrete reason.
\prov{The intervention barely moved the mediator: at the largest dose it
reduced the probe roughness (the unnormalized basis of $\nsharp$) by only
$\sim$\TpsRoughRedPenNTwo\% at $N{=}2$, while also perturbing $|\Sbar|$}. At
these noise scales TPS is too weak, and its effect on $|\Sbar|$ too large, to
test the smoothness account either way. A stronger, amplitude-controlled
action-space intervention is the natural follow-up.

\subsection{Sharpness as a prospective signal}
\label{sec:prospective}

Everything above is concurrent measurement. The practical question is whether
$\nsharp$ \emph{forecasts}: can a practitioner probe at an early checkpoint and
rank configurations without finishing the grid? The pre-registered checkpoint
is 100k steps (10\% of training). \prov{At the config level, $\nsharp$ at 100k
is associated with final score among non-divergent pen configurations
($\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRhoCfgExclM$, permutation $p{=}\ProspectivePermPExclM$,
$n{=}\ProspectiveNCfgExclM$ configs; leave-one-config-out range
\ProspectiveLocoCfgExclM), but pooling all configurations the association
weakens to non-significance ($\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRhoCfg$,
$p{=}\ProspectivePermP$, $n{=}\ProspectiveNCfg$)}. Our pre-registered statistic
pooled seed-runs rather than configs, giving an anticonservative
$\rho_s{=}\ProspectiveRho$ over $n{=}\ProspectiveN$ correlated rows; we report
the config-level permutation test as the honest figure.
Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective} and Table~\ref{tab:prospective} trace the
association across the full probe horizon. The probe costs $\sim$$10^5$ critic
forward passes, which is negligible next to a single training run, so logging it
is cheap even at this signal strength. We do not present it as a selection rule.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Critic ensembles entered value-based RL for variance reduction
\citep{anschel2017averaged}; REDQ \citep{chen2021redq} scaled them to high
UTD continuous control with random $M$-of-$N$ minimization; DroQ
\citep{hiraoka2022droq} replaced most heads with dropout; RLPD
\citep{ball2023efficient} carried the recipe to offline-to-online learning
(cf.\ also \citep{song2023hybrid}) and found dropout an inferior
\emph{substitute} for the ensemble on sparse-reward Adroit. Diversity-centric
accounts (MSG's independent targets \citep{ghasemipour2022msg}, EDAC's gradient
diversification \citep{an2021edac}, SPQR's independence regularizer
\citep{lee2023spqr}) treat head disagreement as the explanatory variable; our
grid separates disagreement from the smoothness of the mean by construction and
\prov{finds the designed contrasts more consistent with smoothness than with
diversity} (EDAC's diversification in fact lowers the action-gradient norm of
the mean critic, which is our $S_\sigma$, so on our reading it already acts as a
smoother wearing a diversity label). Sharpness as flatness-of-minima is
classical in generalization \citep{keskar2016large,foret2021sam}; we transport
it from parameter space to the action space the actor differentiates. Closest
to us, a recent line makes the critic's action-space geometry the operative
quantity for the policy: \citet{lee2026pave} stabilize the $Q$-gradient field
with a critic regularizer and supporting theory, \citet{jain2025savo} show
multimodal $Q$-landscapes trap deterministic-policy-gradient actors in
dexterous manipulation, and \citet{wen2024ro2o} pair a $Q$-ensemble with
explicit smoothness for robust offline-to-online RL. We share their premise but
add no objective of our own; we use sharpness of the ensemble \emph{mean} to
diagnose why the existing RLPD ensemble helps and to separate that smoothing
from the pessimism and diversity axes. Spectral normalization
bounds critic Lipschitz constants globally \citep{bjorck2021spectral}; our
preliminary single-seed spectral-norm probes (App.~\ref{app:spectral}) were
confounded with LayerNorm and are superseded here by target-policy smoothing
\citep{fujimoto2018td3} as the controlled smoothness intervention.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) Sharpness is probed on a fixed offline probe set,
not at $(s,\pi(s))$ along the actor's own trajectory; the actor-side link, for
example agreement of $\nabla_a\Sbar$ across heads at on-policy actions, is
measured indirectly at best, and closing it is the clearest next experiment.
(ii) Scope is sparse-reward Adroit, where critic artifacts dominate; in
preliminary dense-reward runs (halfcheetah, single-seed, April-era harness)
RLPD saturates regardless of configuration, leaving no headroom for any
account to explain. (iii) Three to five seeds support sign-test-level claims
rather than effect sizes. (iv) Sharpness is one mechanism among several: by
itself it does not explain the residual gap between $N{=}2{+}$dropout and
$N{=}10$ at matched $\nsharp$, and it says nothing about exploration.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Our evidence is most consistent with reading the RLPD
ensemble as a \emph{smoother} of the one function the actor touches, $\Sbar$,
more than as a pessimism device or a diversity device.
\prov{This reading unifies dropout's small-$N$ gains and large-$N$ inertness as
one monotone saturation curve and survives a normalization that removes the
$Q$-scale confound, and separates the $M{=}1$ amplitude collapse from
geometry}. Two caveats keep this a hypothesis
rather than a settled mechanism: the cross-config sharpness--score association
is weak once seeds are not double-counted, and our ensemble-free smoothing
intervention (TPS) was too weak a manipulation to adjudicate
(\S\ref{sec:interventions}). The most informative next tests are a stronger
amplitude-controlled smoothing knob and the actor-side link (Limitation~(i)).
The natural target beyond ensembles is flow-based offline-to-online methods
such as FQL \citep{park2025flow}, where structured critics replace the
ensemble: if the smoothness account is right, $\nsharp$ should predict
performance there without any ensemble in sight.

\section{Preliminary spectral-norm probes (superseded)}
\label{app:spectral}

The course report attempted spectral normalization of critic layers,
mapping each weight matrix to $w\,W/\sigma_{\max}(W)$, as a smoothness
intervention. Those runs were single-seed (April era; the sharpness readings
there came from the course report's diagnostic scripts, not the fleet
harness) and structurally confounded:
LayerNorm (which RLPD requires) re-normalizes activations after the spectral
scaling, $w{=}0.5$ vs.\ $1.0$ dissociated sharpness from score, and the dense
reward replication did not move sharpness at all. We report this for
completeness and rely on target-policy smoothing, which composes cleanly with
LayerNorm, as the controlled intervention in this paper. A LayerNorm-free
spectral sweep remains future work.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

JAX implementation on the reference RLPD codebase, one run per GPU allocation:
38 grid and 8 TPS runs on an RTX 4000 Ada (20GB), 24 grid and 16 TPS runs on
half-card A6000 shards; 2.7--7.6 wall-hours per 1M-step run (median 4.7).
Probe overhead $<\!1\%$.
